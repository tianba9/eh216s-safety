Twelve Hazards, One Control Structure, One Fault Tree

Five of twelve hazards are catastrophic. They are controlled by architecture, energy systems and crashworthiness — four different disciplines.

01FHA: Twelve Hazards

Loss of control, total thrust loss, battery fire, mid-air collision and fatal crash injury are catastrophic. Catastrophic targets are 10⁻⁹ per flight hour.

02STPA Control Structure

The control chain crosses from the authority and operator through the ground station and data link to the flight controller and actuators. Unsafe control actions include wrong, late or missing commands.

03Unsafe Control Actions

The most dangerous case is a wrong decision made while the system believes itself healthy: a second switched-in channel that is faulty, or a late remote-crew takeover.

04Fault Tree: Uncommanded Ground Impact

Nine cut sets across attitude loss, thrust loss and structural failure. Three are single points that pierce redundancy.

05Common-Cause Analysis

Software, power, physical installation and maintenance are four shared channels. If the three flight controllers run the same code, 1oo3 collapses to 1oo1.

06Chapter Summary

The weakest point of 2oo3 is two channels failing in the same direction. Public material says “independent” but not whether the software is heterogeneous.
