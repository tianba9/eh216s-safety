Each Method Owns One Question

Five methods divide the work: FHA grades, STPA finds systemic gaps, FTA derives cut sets, FMEA covers parts, CCA checks common causes.

01The Analysis Chain

Each step feeds the next. Doing them out of order causes rework; choosing the wrong one produces tables without design consequences.

02Why STPA Is Required

FTA and FMEA assume a component has failed. The high-risk cases in a pilotless, software-dense architecture occur when every component works but the control logic is unsafe.

03Three Standards Traditions

Aviation (ARP4754A, ARP4761, DO-178C), automotive (ISO 26262, 21448, 21434) and the type-specific condition SC-21-002. Existing rules assume a pilot, so a new condition was required.

04Seven Requirement Categories & Methods

Flight performance, structure and crashworthiness, propulsion, systems, remote-crew interface, data link and ground station each need a distinct form of evidence.

05DAL & Development Rigor

Level A requires MC/DC coverage and independent verification. Three channels must be developed by independent teams or the independence argument fails.

06Chapter Summary

Methods are not interchangeable. FTA without STPA misses “all parts healthy, logic wrong”; redundancy without CCA misses a single shared cause.
