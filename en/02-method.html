Each Method Owns One Question

FHA assigns severity, FTA quantifies, FMEA covers components, STPA finds systemic gaps, and CCA examines common causes. The typical symptom of choosing the wrong method is producing a pile of tables without being able to say which design decision changed as a result.

01The Analysis Chain: Division of Labour

Systems safety analysis is a chain with a definite order. The output of one step is the input of the next; reversing the order leads to rework.

Table 2-1　Division of labour, inputs and outputs of the five methods
MethodQuestion answeredInputOutput
FHAWhich failures are catastrophic, and what are the probability targetsFunction inventory, scenario libraryHazard list, severity and probability targets, DAL
PSSACan the preliminary architecture meet these targetsFHA output, candidate architecturesArchitecture-level safety requirements, redundancy and monitoring requirements
FTAWhat is the probability of the top eventArchitecture, failure-rate dataTop-event probability, critical cut sets, weak points
FMEAHow does a single component failure propagateComponent list, interfacesFailure modes, local effects, detection means
STPACan the control logic become hazardous when all components workControl structure, control algorithmsUnsafe control actions, causal scenarios
CCAIs redundancy pierced by a single common causeArchitecture, installation and power layoutCommon-cause failure paths, isolation measures
SSASummary: have all targets been metAll of the aboveSystem safety assessment conclusion, supporting the TC

This chain has a particular feature on the EH216-S: the function inventory for the FHA comes from the seven requirement categories of the special condition, not from a company-defined list. This lets the authority set the boundary of hazard identification, reducing the risk of “missing a whole class of hazards”.

02Why STPA Is Required

FTA and FMEA are both built on the concept of “failure”: a component breaks, and the system malfunctions. But many of the main risks of a passenger-carrying unmanned aircraft do not come from component failure; they come from the control logic issuing an unsafe command in a particular situation — when all components are sound.

Table 2-2　Differences in coverage between traditional methods and STPA
SituationDetectable by FTA / FMEADetectable by STPA
Motor bearing wear leading to thrust lossYes (component failure)Yes (but not its strength)
All three flight-control channels affected by the same software defectNo (no component failure)Yes (identifies common cause and missing feedback)
Remote crew misjudging under link delay and issuing a wrong commandNo (human factors outside the model)Yes (the controller includes the human)
Autopilot continuously correcting under a strong crosswind until the actuator saturatesNo (all components normal)Yes (inappropriate control action)
Ground station issuing conflicting commands in a multi-aircraft scenarioNo (system-level interaction)Yes (hierarchical control structure)

The last four situations are precisely the high-incidence zone of a “pilotless + highly automated” architecture. Therefore Chapter 03 builds an STPA control structure for the EH216-S and lists the unsafe control actions item by item.

Basis for choosing methods

When a system is dominated by mechanical and electronic components with enumerable failure modes, FTA/FMEA are sufficient; when a system contains software-intensive autonomous decisions and the human is out of the loop or the loop delay is large, STPA must be added. The EH216-S belongs to the latter.

03Three Standards Traditions

The safety standards for flying vehicles come from three traditions that cannot simply replace one another. Understanding their differences explains why this type must follow the “special condition” route.

Table 2-3　The three standards traditions and what they apply to
TraditionRepresentative standardsApplies toRelevance to this type
AviationSAE ARP4754A / ARP4761, RTCA DO-178C, DO-254, DO-160GDevelopment, safety assessment, hardware/software and environmental testing of civil aircraft and systemsThe backbone of aircraft- and system-level safety analysis
AutomotiveISO 26262, ISO 21448, ISO/SAE 21434Functional safety, SOTIF and cybersecurity of road vehiclesProvides transferable analytical frameworks and terminology
SpecificSC-21-002 special conditionUnmanned passenger-carrying aircraft systemsThe statutory certification basis for this type

Why can't existing standards be used directly? Because traditional airworthiness regulations assume “a pilot on board”. Once the pilot is removed, the three objects — “remote-crew interface”, “data link” and “ground control station” — have no corresponding clauses in the old rules and must be newly established. The special condition was created precisely for this — it is the world's first airworthiness technical framework for unmanned passenger-carrying aircraft.

04Seven Requirement Categories & Methods

SC-21-002 divides certification requirements into seven categories. Each requires a specific form of evidence, and this mapping table is the analytical backbone of the whole site.

Table 2-4　Correspondence between the seven requirement categories and the analysis methods
No.Requirement categoryCore requirement (summarised from public material)Principal means of demonstration
1Flight performanceHover stability, emergency landing capability, envelope limitsPerformance flight tests + envelope verification + simulation
2Structure, design and constructionStructural strength and stiffness; new structural-protection (crashworthiness) requirements for the unmanned passenger cabinStatic and fatigue tests + crash tests + structural analysis
3PowerplantBattery-system thermal-runaway protection and redundant power-supply requirementsFTA + FMEA + thermal-runaway initiation and propagation tests
4Systems and equipmentSystem safety and continuing airworthiness (verification criteria for autonomous diagnosis and maintenance systems)FHA + SSA + FMEA + diagnostic-function verification
5Remote-crew interface and other informationInterface design, flight manual, continuing-airworthiness documentationHuman-factors evaluation + interface verification + document review
6Data linkTriple-redundant communication architecture and anti-interference criteriaFTA + link-failure tests + EMC tests
7Ground control stationReal-time monitoring and emergency takeover functionsFunctional verification + failure drills + human factors

Three bolded entries deserve attention: crashworthiness, thermal runaway and the triple-redundant link. These are hard requirements absent from traditional unmanned-aircraft certification, added because of “carrying people”, and are the focus of Chapters 04 and 06 of this report.

An overlooked detail

Category 4 includes “continuing airworthiness (verification criteria for autonomous diagnosis and maintenance systems)”. This means the self-diagnostic capability itself must be verified — it is not enough to claim “the system can find its own faults”; one must show which faults it can find and what its missed-detection rate is. On traditional aircraft this is usually covered by maintenance programmes and manual inspection; under an unmanned architecture it is partly transferred to the on-board system.

05DAL & Development Rigor

The Development Assurance Level (DAL) determines the rigour of hardware and software development and verification. The higher the level, the stricter the required independence, coverage and documentation.

Table 2-5　DAL and hardware/software development requirements
DALFailure consequenceSoftware requirement (DO-178C analogue)Hardware requirement (DO-254 analogue)
ACatastrophicMC/DC coverage, independent verification, formal methods optionalFull lifecycle, independent verification
BHazardousDecision coverage, independent verificationFull lifecycle
CMajorStatement coverage, requirements traceabilityPartial lifecycle
DMinorBasic traceabilityBasic verification

According to the safety-objective allocation in Chapter 01 of this report, flight control, energy management and fire control must reach Level A, meaning their software must meet the highest coverage and independent-verification requirements. This also explains why the three flight-control channels must be developed by independent teams with independent tool chains — otherwise they cannot pass the Level A independence review, and the acceptability of common-cause failure cannot be argued.

Limitations of this report's method

Without a failure-rate database and internal architecture data, the FTA in this report can only be semi-quantitative: it gives cut sets and critical paths, not precise probabilities. A quantitative treatment would require the manufacturer's FMEA data and operating statistics from similar types.

06Chapter Summary

The five methods each own one part: FHA grades severity, STPA finds systemic gaps, FTA derives cut sets, FMEA covers components, and CCA checks common causes. The wrong order causes rework; the wrong method produces a pile of tables without saying which design changed as a result.

Why STPA is required: FTA and FMEA are built on “component failure”, whereas the high-incidence risk of an unmanned architecture is “all components sound, logic wrong”. The differences among the three standards traditions also explain why a special condition had to be established.
