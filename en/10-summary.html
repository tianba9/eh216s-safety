Valid Inside the ODD, Unargued Outside

Taken apart over nine chapters, safety is not piled up from redundancy but stands on two legs: one is design — “it can still land after failure” — and the other is operations — “it is not allowed to take risks”. The former can be proved by engineering; the latter can only be constrained by regulation. Neglect either and the argument collapses.

01One Thread: Responsibility First

The order of analysis is: first distribute safety responsibility clearly among four parties, then define what is flown, and only then hazards, architecture, hardware and software, physical safety, verification and the argument.

02Three Key Findings

1. Removing the pilot is the first-principle choice

It eliminates fatigue, misoperation and spatial disorientation, while also removing the last on-board backstop. The task of all subsequent safety design is to compensate for this — which is why the remote-crew interface and ground control station are listed as a separate certification requirement.

2. Redundancy solves random failures, not common causes

The 2oo3 voting of three flight-control sets can tolerate one erroneous channel, provided at most one is wrong. If the three run the same software, share a power supply or occupy the same bay, 1oo3 degrades to 1oo1 at that moment. Public material says only “independent”, not “heterogeneous” — this is the point this report most wants to question.

3. The safety boundary is the operating boundary

Segregated airspace, visual meteorological conditions, daytime operation and crowd segregation — each controls systemic risk while also compressing market space. The conservatism of the safety argument directly determines that today it can only do sightseeing at scenic sites.

03Three Undisclosed Gaps

  • Whether the three flight-control sets are heterogeneous. Software of common origin would defeat voting in the face of a defect, directly affecting the basis for arguing catastrophic-failure probability.
  • Quantified crashworthiness criteria. Impact speed, deceleration limits and energy-absorbing stroke are all undisclosed, so it is impossible to judge how the 10-9 target for crash fatalities is met.
  • Remote-crew ratio and takeover time limit. Human factors are the active element in the link and may become a new bottleneck when multiple aircraft operate.

Identifying gaps is not challenging the certification conclusion. The full certification material circulates only between the authority and the company; the point of listing them is that, if operations are to be extended to new scenarios, these three are the parts needing reinforcement first.

04Final Position

This aircraft is safe not because it has a lot of redundancy, but because it is allowed to fly only within narrow conditions. Redundancy makes it reliable within the conditions; operating limits keep it from being outside them.

R2

Release level: restricted-scope release

Valid only within the ODD — segregated airspace, visual meteorological conditions, daytime operation, crowd segregation at the vertiport — and must be executed by an OC-holding operator under continuing oversight. Conditions outside the ODD are unargued and cannot invoke this report's conclusions.

If this report were compressed into one sentence: design determines how safe it is when something goes wrong; operating limits determine how often something goes wrong — the latter is more easily overlooked and more easily eroded by commercial pressure.
