Redundancy Is Designing What Happens After Failure

Three flight-control channels, twelve battery groups and three data links form layered redundancy. Its value lies in what happens after a failure.

01Architecture Overview

Control, energy and communications are each redundant; fail-safe logic and a remote crew form the outer layers. No single layer covers every failure.

02Choosing a Redundancy Scheme

2oo3 voting balances safety and availability, but only if at most one channel is wrong. Two channels failing in the same direction defeats the vote.

03Voting & Degradation Paths

A single disagreeing channel is isolated; a link loss triggers autonomous fail-safe behaviour; unrecoverable states trigger an emergency landing.

04Focus: Flight-Control Redundancy

With 500 N available per rotor, single-rotor and single-arm failures remain controllable. Losing two arms leaves 6,000 N against a 6,082 N demand — the tolerance boundary.

05Fault-Tolerance Boundary & Residual Risk

Tolerance is not “still flying” but “landing under control”. Common-cause and human factors remain outside what hardware can solve.

06Chapter Summary

Redundancy is not a count. The exception path must be designed, detected and verified, or the extra channels are decorative.
