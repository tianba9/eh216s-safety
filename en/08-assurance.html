01Four Evidence Chains

The four GSN sub-goals correspond to four independent evidence chains. If any one breaks, the top-level claim fails — this is “all must hold”, not a “weighted average”.

G2 Hazard identification

Available basis
This report documents 12 FHA hazards and maps causes with STPA and fault-tree analysis.
Still to verify
The manufacturer’s common-cause conclusions and coverage are unavailable.
Inspect the hazard analysis

G3 Architecture

Available basis
Public material describes control, battery and link redundancy. The static model estimates remaining total thrust after a failure.
Still to verify
Attitude control, emergency landing and shared software failures require verification evidence.
Inspect the thrust model

G4 Verification

Available basis
Certificates document approval outcomes. The report identifies methods such as rig and hardware-in-the-loop tests.
Still to verify
Public material is insufficient to review fault-injection results and occupant crash criteria.
Inspect the verification matrix

G5 Operating constraints

Available basis
Operator certification can be checked. Flight counts come from the company.
Still to verify
Takeover timing, crew-to-aircraft ratios and independent operational deviation data remain unavailable.
Inspect the operating evidence

The assessment concludes: the argument is formally complete but incomplete in verifiability. This is not a rejection of the type — certification details belong between the regulator and the company, and external access is the exception. What must be distinguished is: which claims have public evidence, and which can only rely on the mere fact of “having been certified”.

02Three Gaps in the Argument

Based on the analyses in the preceding chapters, this report identifies three gaps in this type's safety argument that deserve questioning. They are not “defects” but points that public information cannot close.

Table 8-3　The three gaps and their impact
GapWhy it mattersConsequence if true
① Are the three flight-control sets heterogeneous Software of common origin would degrade 2oo3 to 1oo1 in the face of a software defect, directly affecting G3 The basis for arguing catastrophic-failure probability is weakened; must be compensated by a stricter development process
② Quantified crashworthiness criteria The special condition added cabin structural-protection requirements, but impact speed, deceleration limits and energy-absorbing stroke are all undisclosed Public material is insufficient to verify whether H05 (fatal injury to occupants in a crash) meets the catastrophic-event target adopted by this report (≤ 10-9 per flight hour).
③ Remote-crew ratio and takeover time limit Human factors are the active element in the link, and their reliability directly determines the ultimate effectiveness of the fail-safe When multiple aircraft operate in the same area, takeover capability may become a new bottleneck
The right attitude towards the gaps

Identifying gaps is not the same as challenging the certification conclusion. The full certification material circulates only between the authority and the company, and external invisibility is the institutional norm. The point of listing them here is: if this safety argument is to be used for new scenarios (such as urban commuting), these three are precisely the parts needing reinforcement first — because new scenarios amplify their consequences.

R2

Report assessment: restricted-scope release

Valid only within the ODD — segregated airspace, visual meteorological conditions, daytime operation, crowd segregation at the vertiport — and must be executed by an OC-holding operator under continuing oversight. Conditions outside the ODD are unargued and cannot invoke this report's conclusions. The basis for the rating is in Chapters 07 and 08.

Judgement 1

Within the defined ODD, the main body of the safety argument holds

Hazard identification is complete, the architecture is clearly layered, verification activities are in place and operating constraints are explicit. The existence of the four certificates shows that this argument chain has been reviewed and accepted by the regulator.

Judgement 2

The argument's strength is functional safety; its weakness is passive safety

The design logic of redundant architecture and fail-safe is rigorous, but quantitative evidence for crash protection and fire response is the thinnest. This is consistent with the product strategy of “certification before performance” — first solve “nothing goes wrong”, then solve “less harm when something goes wrong”.

Judgement 3

The safety boundary and the commercial boundary are the same line

Segregated airspace, visual meteorological conditions, daytime operation and crowd segregation — each operating limit both controls systemic risk and compresses market space. The conservatism of the safety argument directly determines that today it can only do sightseeing at scenic sites.

In one sentence

This aircraft is safe not “because it has a lot of redundancy” but “because it is allowed to fly only within narrow conditions”. Redundancy makes it reliable within the conditions; operating limits keep it from being outside them. Both are indispensable, and the latter is more easily overlooked.

05What Each Certificate Proves

For a third party, the four certificates are the only available signal that “the argument has been reviewed”. Understanding which segment each certificate covers is necessary to judge how much evidence it can replace.

Table 8-2　Scope of the argument covered by each of the four certificates
CertificateObject coveredWhat it provesWhat it does not prove
TCType designThe design meets the seven categories of the special conditionDoes not guarantee individual-aircraft quality and does not cover operations
ACIndividual aircraftThat aircraft is in an airworthy conditionDoes not cover subsequent maintenance or modification deviations
PCProduction systemHas series-production consistency and quality-control capabilityDoes not guarantee every aircraft is defect-free
OCOperatorHas the personnel, procedures and support capability for continuing safe operationDoes not cover airspace risk on specific routes

The four certificates form a complete chain of responsibility: design — individual aircraft — production — operation. This is also why the OC is held by the operator rather than the manufacturer — it fixes “who is flying”, not “who built it”.

06Chapter Summary

GSN splits the argument into four sub-goals that must all hold; if any one breaks, the top-level claim fails. The four evidence chains are formally complete but incomplete in verifiability — normal for a third-party view and the reason this report repeatedly marks items as “undisclosed”.

Three gaps deserve questioning: whether the three flight-control sets are heterogeneous, the quantified crashworthiness criteria, and the remote-crew ratio and takeover time limit. Release level R2, valid only within the ODD.
