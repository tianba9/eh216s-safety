Verification Proves Requirements, Not Flight Hours

Every layer of the V-model needs matching evidence. Cumulative flight hours cannot replace that.

01The V-Model & Verification Chain

Requirements, architecture and detailed design on the left; component, integration, vehicle and operational verification on the right. A missing layer means an unproved requirement.

02Choosing Means of Compliance

Eight means exist, from design review to flight test. Catastrophic scenarios cannot be verified by actually causing them.

03Verification Matrix

Each safety requirement maps to a method and a pass criterion: voting, isolation, thermal propagation, link loss, crash deceleration and remote takeover.

04Fault Injection & Scenario Testing

Single motor, dual motor, link loss, sensor bias, GNSS spoofing and voting disagreement must be injected deliberately.

05Statistical Weight of Operating Evidence

90,000 sorties equal roughly 37,500 flight hours — four orders below what a 10⁻⁹ demonstration requires. It is reliability-growth evidence, not proof.

06Blind Spots in Verification

Concurrent failures, long-term degradation and extreme weather cannot be exhaustively tested; they are handled by ODD limits and periodic inspection.

07Chapter Summary

Verification answers “was each requirement met”. Flight hours answer “how often did nothing go wrong”. Confusing the two is the most common error in safety argumentation.
