Define Responsibility, Then Mission

Responsibility is redistributed among four certificate holders; the passenger on board is entirely passive. That constraint shapes every later design decision.

01Safety Culture & Responsibility Allocation

Design owns the safety case, manufacturing owns consistency, the operator owns procedures, and the authority owns certification and oversight. Four certificates allocate responsibility across four parties.

02Vehicle Function Inventory

Ten functions F1–F10 cover take-off, autonomous route flight, attitude holding, energy distribution, health monitoring, fail-safe behaviour, communications, remote takeover, occupant protection and precision landing.

03Operating Design Conditions

Airspace, weather, vertiport, daylight, population density, communications and electromagnetic environment form the design boundary. Every condition is a limit on the safety case.

04Scenario Library

Six scenarios S1–S6 range from scenic circuits to multi-aircraft operations. Urban sightseeing is the densest hazard environment.

05Function–Scenario–Hazard Matrix

Attitude control, energy and occupant protection are critical in every scenario; the urban scenario concentrates the most risks.

06Chapter Summary

Define who is responsible before discussing hazards. With no pilot on board, the passenger cannot help; that assumption runs through the whole report.
