The Two Worst Outcomes: Fire and Impact

Catastrophic consequences take two physical forms. Thermal runaway has four break points; crash energy has a hard numerical limit.

01Focus A: Battery Thermal Runaway

Triggers are mechanical, electrical or thermal. The chain runs from cell heating to separator melt, runaway, venting and spread to neighbouring groups.

02Runaway Chain & Break Points

Early warning, cell isolation, group-level barriers and directed venting. The goal is not “never happens” but “never leaves one group”.

03Focus B: Crash Safety

Structure must absorb impact energy without exceeding human tolerance. At 8 m/s about 0.3 m of crush distance is required.

04Energy Absorption, Quantified

At 12 m/s no practical stroke keeps deceleration within tolerance. Passive safety therefore cannot stand alone.

05High-Voltage Electrical & EMC

Insulation, interlocks, fusing and electromagnetic compatibility. Sixteen switching power stages make self-interference a real risk.

06Fire Protection & Materials

With no pilot, no disclosed extinguishing system and a small cabin, the only viable fire strategy is preventing propagation.

07Chapter Summary

Fire must be contained within one group; impact must remain below the speed where structure can no longer protect the occupant. Both have quantitative limits.
