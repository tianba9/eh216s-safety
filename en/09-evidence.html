The Evidence Base

Safety conclusions place higher demands on sources than performance data: a single misquoted failure rate can invalidate the conclusions of an entire fault tree. This page lists all sources, referenced standards and calculation assumptions for readers to check.

04Limitations

  • No internal material. This report has not obtained the type's FHA, PSSA, SSA or FMEA reports or compliance-verification documents; all analysis is reconstructed from public information, constituting an “independent third-party recomputation” and not equivalent to the manufacturer's formal safety assessment.
  • No quantitative probability. Lacking failure-rate data and architecture details, the fault tree gives only cut sets and a qualitative ranking, not a top-event probability.
  • No inference about undisclosed configurations. For the whole-aircraft parachute, on-board extinguishing system, power-supply topology and software heterogeneity — whatever is not stated in public material is marked “undisclosed” rather than “absent”.
  • Operating data unaudited. The 90,000 sorties and zero accidents are company figures; this report uses them at the lowest credibility level and explains their statistical limitations in Chapter 07.
  • Standard versions may lag. The ARP4754A, DO-178C and other standards cited here are current versions; in actual certification the authority may adopt newer or equivalent acceptable methods.
On the methodological stance of this report

The value of safety analysis lies not in giving a “safe” or “unsafe” conclusion but in breaking “why it is considered safe” into questionable steps. The purpose of identifying three gaps in this report is not to challenge the certification conclusion, but to mark the places needing the most reinforcement when operating scenarios are extended.

05Chapter Summary

This chapter presents all the evidence, the referenced standards, the calculation assumptions and the limitations statement. Sixteen sources are arranged by type and credibility, each noting the claim it supports; the absence of failure-rate data directly determines that the fault tree gives only a qualitative ranking.

It defines the scope of the conclusions: this is an independent third-party recomputation, not a certification finding; any undisclosed configuration is marked “undisclosed” and not inferred to be “absent”.
